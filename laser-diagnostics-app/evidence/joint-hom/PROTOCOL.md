# Sequential emitter/environment and joint-photon instrument

Specified before numerical experiments in this new folder. Earlier dot/HOM data are frozen and are not fitted here. This is a bounded local modeling study; future IBM execution would emulate this optical model with superconducting qubits, not measure a quantum dot or optical interference apparatus.

## Gap and physical model

The earlier model supplied single-pair overlaps but no conditional instrument on retained memory. Build that instrument explicitly. A selected two-level emitter is ideally excited once, fully radiatively decays, and returns to its ground state before the next excitation. For fixed environmental state z, its photon has temporal amplitude `phi_w(t)=sqrt(Gamma)*exp(-Gamma*t/2-i*w*t)`, t>=0. The source/environment isometry is `|e,z,vac> -> |g,z,phi_(w_z)>`, derived from radiative decay and the dispersive excited-state shift `H/hbar=delta*Z_environment*|e><e|`. Complete decay removes the emitter's independent state from the retained memory; this is an explicit full-emission limit, not the original weakly driven emitter's arbitrary dynamics.

Use T1=1 ns, Gamma=1/T1, delta=Gamma in **angular frequency units**. Environment basis states give shifts −delta,+delta. The environment remains fixed during each photon wavepacket. Between emissions apply `U=Ry(theta)`, theta=pi/3 per gap; compare coherent evolution with the same U followed by complete Z dephasing. The latter is an exact classical two-state transition with flip probability sin²(theta/2). These are deliberately simplified, competing models of gap dynamics, not measured charge-trap physics. Gap rotations are specified operations; no gap duration or microscopic switching rate is inferred. Fast bath dephasing, imperfect excitation, multiphoton emission, nonradiative decay and motion during a wavepacket are excluded.

The initial memory is I/2. Retain it across repeated nonoverlapping two-photon trials. Apply the gap operation between the two emissions and between trials. Report the final conditional memory immediately after the last joint observation, before any further gap. A known second-pulse source-frequency intervention u is either0 (passive) or +Gamma/−Gamma. This idealization represents a calibrated transition-frequency shift during that emission, with equal excitation and collection imposed. Its feasibility and spectral selectivity on a real dot need measurement.

## Exact finite mode representation and measurement

For any chosen setting, the frequencies `{−delta,+delta,u−delta,u+delta}` define at most four exponential wavepackets. Their Gram matrix is `G_ij=<phi_i|phi_j>=Gamma/[Gamma+i*(w_j−w_i)]`. A factor B satisfying B†B=G embeds their **full temporal states** in an orthonormal space of dimension rank(G). This is exact for the stated finite family; it is not time discretization or a fitted coincidence-probability rotation. Preserve complex phases: the amplitude Gram matrix, not only squared overlap, determines the joint memory instrument. Remove only numerical eigenvalues below1e−12; record eigenvalues and reconstruction error. Pad mode dimension to a power of two for circuit encoding.

Build the two-emission isometry with retained environmental state. On its two photon registers use symmetric and antisymmetric projectors `(I±SWAP)/2`. Antisymmetric probability is the ideal balanced-HOM opposite-port coincidence probability. Trace out the measured photons to obtain completely positive maps on the retained memory. For correlated photons the relevant quantity is Tr(SWAP*rho_pair); do not substitute a product of single-photon marginals or call it a general single-photon purity. A literal bosonic beamsplitter calculation independently checks the projection and conditional memory maps.

The known equivalence between HOM and SWAP measurements is prior work: [Garcia-Escartin and Chamorro-Posada, PRA87,052330](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.6814v1). [Thoma et al., PRL116,033601](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05900) motivates temporal and environmental controls; our channel and parameters are not their experimental fit. The original QRSM's conditional hidden-state update is reused as a modeling principle, without changing the original files.

## Acquisition and nuisance model

Use nominal independent per-photon survival eta=.8. The registered outcome alphabet is no usable pair / bunch / coincidence. Ideal number-resolving detection or equivalent reliable total-pair accounting is assumed: two photons in the same output must still count as a usable pair. The no-pair map is `(1−eta²)` times the unconditional source channel. Detected maps carry eta². Binary outcome confusion is e01=.01 (bunch→coincidence), e10=.02 (coincidence→bunch). The conditional memory update mixes the corresponding true branches. Optical mode mismatch is represented by a separate known-overlap parameter mu; its coin map is `[(1+mu)/2]*J_antisymmetric + [(1−mu)/2]*J_symmetric`. Nominal mu=1. This is a declared independent mismatch channel, not a unique physical diagnosis.
