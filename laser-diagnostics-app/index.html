WHAT THIS ADDS TO REWIND & AMPLIFY

A better physical model starts with the right observations.

Three lessons from this work

  1. Choose the observable. The toy sources have identical brightness and g⁽²⁾(0), yet photon overlap ranges from 20% to 100%. Counts alone miss useful state information.
  2. Keep the pair history. The same single-photon frequency distribution gives 100% or 60% overlap when the classical pair correlations change.
  3. Model the apparatus. At 39.4 ns, two normalizations of the same external data give 77.24% and 79.38%. That tests a measurement assumption; it does not identify a defect.

WHAT DOES “CLICK” MEAN?

A click is a recorded 1 in one output time bin; 0 means no event was recorded. In the optical interpretation, it is an on/off light detector’s event.

The IBM experiment uses superconducting qubits to represent this record. It did not detect optical photons. A single click does not identify a defect.

What the QRSM adds

For each candidate physical model, the QRSM carries a hidden quantum-state estimate between steps. Each recorded bit conditions that estimate through the model’s measurement update, including backaction. It then predicts later-bit probabilities. Classical fitting can compare interaction-and-noise models on records that were not used for fitting.

Why defects matter for state estimates

An unseen interaction can change a quantum state and its later readout statistics. Readout errors can imitate those changes. Mistaking one cause for the other can bias an inferred state or coupling, so the analysis needs independent calibration and controls.

The intended benefit is physics-shaped structure; classical sequence models also capture memory. Earlier fitting used synthetic records. Current IBM circuits implement known models. The hidden state remains an inference, not a direct state measurement or tomography result, and no quantum advantage is established here.
