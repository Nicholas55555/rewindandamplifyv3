Grover is famously fragile. Each iteration rewinds the whole history and checks every qubit. At 0.2% error per gate, only about 40% of those runs finish clean.

The resilient architecture: four moves

1 Purify

Fresh qubits replace resets, so the machine can be rewound.

Price: one qubit per step

2 Steer

Tune one gate so the event is common. Free, and exact.

4,400× fewer runs on a real record

3 Amplify locally

One iteration, with a reflection on 3 qubits at any length.

With steering: 3 to 5× fewer gates

4 Verify

The click is measured, so a failed shot shows itself and is dropped.

On a real chip: 87% of shots kept

Now the honest problem. Grover is famously fragile, because every iteration rewinds the whole history and checks every qubit. Compiled for an IBM chip, the textbook version at five steps needs 456 two-qubit gates. At today’s error rates, most of those runs have a mistake in them. We don’t need this to be practical on today’s chips. But it made us ask a better question: what would a version built around noise look like? We ended up with four moves. Purify you’ve seen. Steer, and amplify locally, are new, and they’re the next two slides.
